# The ferry stops running. What do the notice and the diary each tell us?

[简体中文](text-reading.md) · [Back to the drafting prompts](../../README.en.md#drafting)

Imagine a classmate describes the first passage as “objective” and the second as “personal.” What words on the page led them there? Both passages were written for this exercise. Copy the whole box to try it.

```text
Write a paragraph of 80–110 words for a university writing class. Compare how the two passages describe the ferry closure. Pay attention to how each marks time and who acts in each passage. Quote at least one short phrase from each passage exactly as written and explain what it shows. Use only the material below.

Passage 1 | Ferry notice (fictional)
From 1 October, the South Bay ferry will no longer run. Passengers should take the number 3 bus and get off at East Bridge.

Passage 2 | Passenger’s diary (fictional)
1 October. My alarm went off at ten past six as usual. I made it to the end of the lane before I slipped the ferry ticket back into my pocket and turned to wait for the number 3.
```

<details>
<summary>See one possible reading and check the claim</summary>

The notice uses “From 1 October” to tell passengers when to change routes, then names the bus and the stop. The diarist dates the entry the same day but gives the time down to the minute. The alarm rings “as usual” that morning. At the end of the lane the passenger slips the ferry ticket back into a pocket before turning to wait for the bus. The notice gives the new route in a sentence. For this passenger, the switch takes place partway through the morning walk.

**Check the claim**

Try replacing “this passenger” with “everyone in town” in the last sentence. The diary follows one person down the lane. Whose account would you need to make a claim about other passengers?
