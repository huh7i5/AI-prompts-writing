# Why did the library’s monthly attendance rate fall when attendance rose in both evening slots?

[简体中文](reading-room-paper.md) · [Source data and exercise prompt](reading-room.en.md)

*Sample paper for teaching. All library bookings in this example are fictional.*

## Abstract

The library recorded 100 evening bookings in each of two months. Check-ins fell from 82 to 63, although the attendance rate rose by five percentage points in each time slot. The later slot had the lower attendance rate and accounted for one fifth of bookings in March and four fifths in April. Reporting booking and check-in counts by time slot would let readers judge whether attendance improved for a given part of the evening.

## Introduction

A fall in monthly attendance might lead a library to conclude that readers have become more likely to miss their bookings at any given hour. Can a monthly total support that conclusion? We used two evening time slots to compare the trend for each slot with the trend for the same records pooled by month.

## Data and methods

Each booking covered one seat in one time slot. Four records gave the numbers of bookings and check-ins by month and slot. For each slot, we divided check-ins by bookings. For each monthly rate, we added the check-ins across both slots and divided by the total bookings for that month.

We also calculated April attendance with the March booking mix held fixed. For this comparison, we weighted each April slot rate by the share of March bookings in that slot.

## Results

| Time slot | March check-ins / bookings | March rate | April check-ins / bookings | April rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 6–8 pm | 72 / 80 | 90% | 19 / 20 | 95% |
| 8–10 pm | 10 / 20 | 50% | 44 / 80 | 55% |
| Both slots | 82 / 100 | 82% | 63 / 100 | 63% |

The early slot accounted for 80% of March bookings and 20% of April bookings. Applying the April slot rates to the March booking shares gave 0.80 × 95% + 0.20 × 55% = **87%**, five percentage points above the March rate of 82%. With those same slot rates, changing the weights to the April shares brought the result down by 24 percentage points to the observed 63%.

## Discussion and conclusion

The monthly rate answers a practical question for the library. Out of every 100 bookings, how many resulted in a check-in? To tell whether a booking was more likely to result in a check-in at a given time of evening, the library also needs the counts by slot. In this example, attendance improved within both slots while a growing share of bookings fell in the slot with lower attendance.

The four records contain no reader identifiers, daily seat availability or accounts of why readers chose their slots. They show how bookings were distributed across the evening and how that distribution affected the monthly figure. To establish whether individual readers switched slots, or why they did so, the library would need additional records.
