The draft needs a small structural revision: retain the distinction between the observed workload problem and the proposed trial, remove repetition, and make the checks required before expansion the report’s main conclusion. The available observations support investigating deduplication; they do not establish that it works.

The current sections serve these roles:

| Location | Reader’s question | Role in the argument | Evidence or basis |

These sections address a common question: **What do the pilot observations justify doing next, and what must be checked before deciding whether to expand?** For a formal partner report, findings followed by a bounded recommendation answer that question directly. No required framework has been supplied, so there is no reason to introduce a regional-to-local structure or another external framework.

The priorities are:

1. **Make the expansion decision explicit in sections 1 and 2.** Section 1 reports a workload problem and acknowledges that no solution is validated; the recommendations propose deduplication. The missing link is an explicit statement that the proposed trial must inform the decision about expansion. Keep the observation in section 1, then open section 2 with the need to check duplicates and incorrect merges before deciding whether to expand. Do not imply demonstrated performance gains.

2. **Merge the duplicated recommendations in sections 1.1.1.1 and 2.** Their exact repetition creates two locations for the same action and obscures which recommendation governs. Retain the material once, under section 2. Move the source-checking instruction from section 1.1 into the same section, before the proposed trial. Keep the administrator’s confirmation of access permission as a prerequisite.

3. **Flatten the hierarchy and replace process labels.** “Connection to the previous section” describes a writing function rather than its subject; “Further thoughts” hides a substantive recommendation. The jump to 1.1.1.1 also suggests intermediate sections that are absent from the supplied draft. Delete those nested headings after moving their useful content. Two main sections are sufficient: “Pilot findings and limitations” and “Proposed trial and checks before expansion.” Both headings describe substantive parts of the argument at a comparable level of detail.

The resulting revision plan is minimal: section 1 keeps the workload finding and the limitation on validation; section 2 combines source checking, access confirmation, the single-collection trial, the recording of omissions and incorrect merges, and the expansion decision. Delete the repeated finding and the second copy of the recommendation.

A short revised passage would read:

> **1 Pilot findings and limitations**
>
> The small pilot found that duplicate records increased comparison workload during retrieval. No solution has been validated, and no quantitative performance results are available.
>
> **2 Proposed trial and checks before expansion**
>
> Before deciding whether to expand the pilot, check duplicate records and assess incorrect merges through a deduplication trial in one collection. The collection administrator must first confirm access permission. Check source records before starting the trial, and record omissions and incorrect merges during it. Use these observations to inform the decision about expansion.

Two points require evidence or an author decision rather than editorial repair:

- Any claim that deduplication reduces workload or improves retrieval needs supporting results. Until those are available, retain it as a proposed intervention.
- The supplied material does not define acceptable levels of omissions or incorrect merges. The authors and partner need to specify how the trial’s findings will guide an expansion decision; the revision should not invent those criteria.
